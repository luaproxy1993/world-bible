# Canon — what to steal

This file is a **core rule book**: a toy box. Steal chapter *moves*
from these books, not their worlds or their math.

A core book has: the world today, who you can be, how a fight works here,
wages and prices, who can hurt you, famous faces, a city tour, gear, a
timeline. It does **not** have a plotted campaign. That is another book
(`PIPELINE.md`).

Load when prose goes generic, or when the author names one of these documents.
Light, wardrobe, and faces follow the lock.

## D&D Basic Rules (why Baldur's Gate works)

Moves:

- One core book is the shared substrate. Baldur's Gate 1, 2, and 3 are
  later stories that pick races, classes, a city, and monsters from that box.
- Character options, a conflict procedure, a default setting tour, famous
  names, gear. Not a plotted campaign in the core book.
- Thick enough that three authors can cut three different stories without
  inventing a new magic system.

Do not: paste D&D races, classes, hit points, or Forgotten Realms names
into an original lock.

## Cyberpunk Red Core Rulebook

Moves:

- Open with fiction that *is* the temperament, then the primer
- History as **residue** on the present (the Time of the Red, not a textbook)
- A default city walked as a tour: districts, gangs, named locations a GM can run tonight
- Everyday life as its own chapter
- Roles as "who you can be on this stage"
- Local slang at a dozen words, not a dictionary
- Laws of the street that are gameplay (*Style over Substance*)

Do not: dump 200 years of dates; write Night City fanfic into an original lock.

## World of Darkness (V20)

Moves:

- A World of Darkness: the kindred *and the world they hunt* in one chapter
- Sects as relationship maps, not org charts
- Traditions: world rules players can break
- Metaplot left in the players' hands (setting-agnostic events)
- Clans as playable temperaments inside one stage

Do not: ship Disciplines, dots, or a full splat. A Law is a commandment, not a power list.

## GURPS (worldbooks + campaign bible)

Moves:

- Genre book ≠ worldbook. This skill writes a worldbook's lore half
- Campaign bible is an almanac of *this campaign*, not the planet. Including a legendary spear creates the expectation it will appear
- Scope: start at the playable present, then branch
- "The Book" (TV writers' bible): what has happened + unused background, consistent and linkable

Do not: generate GURPS stats, point-buy, or a planet-design sequence unless the author asked for that game.

## Edith Finch original concept doc

Moves:

- Cover image that *is* the tone
- One paragraph hook before any synopsis
- **Named feelings** (Sublime / Intimate / Murky) plus "what we mean by X"
- Story cards: `Name, age, year` + one incident. Shared characters across stories
- Specific place, not generic fog (*this is Washington*)
- Tone references as a short list of works, not a moodboard dump

Do not: copy the Finch curse, Orcas Island, or their story list.

## Diablo 1994 pitch (Condor)

Moves:

- Plot in one breath that already implies systems (town above, dungeon below, descent)
- Spatial temperament: church → crypt → demonic halls
- A walkthrough of one session so the world is playable in the mind
- Expandability stated (new creatures / items / halls), not a sequel bible
- Period images that lock the look without a full art team

Do not: write a marketing section, a 12-month Gantt, or Diablo fanfic.

## Signature class (user example)

Dishonored's whale oil: a visible resource that lights the city, runs the tech, smells, can be stolen, and tells you who has power. Every WB names one thing of this class. If removing it does not change Everyday life and Laws, it is decoration — rewrite.
